1. DECISION RULE
======================================================================

This document resolves technical design choices wherever the existing Kawad Swad
policy sources provide enough direction. It does not invent accounting, tax,
employment, approval-limit or other business policies that require Owner, CA,
legal or other professional authority.

Use these labels:
RESOLVED DESIGN: recommended technical rule is definite.
CONDITIONALLY RESOLVED: default is defined, but a named authority must confirm
a specific business or professional matter.
PENDING EVIDENCE: decision depends on source inventory or live configuration evidence.

The governing policy source remains the Kawad Swad ERP Complete Master Policy
Constitution v1.2, subject to Owner adoption. Google Sheets remains the core
business source of truth. Apps Script owns governed business writes. FastAPI
authenticates, authorizes, reads and forwards signed requests. React is UI only.
No historical business records are to be restored.

======================================================================
2. O-01: TARGET ARCHITECTURE
STATUS: RESOLVED DESIGN
======================================================================

Adopt this architecture as the target:

1. Google Sheets:
   Sole authoritative core business database.

2. Apps Script:
   Sole governed business-write owner for canonical business transactions.
   It validates, applies approved business commands, allocates IDs, records
   required audit evidence, and verifies durable readback.

3. FastAPI:
   Authentication, server-side role/permission checks, read/query APIs,
   request validation, signed forwarding to Apps Script, and response handling.
   FastAPI must not write business records directly to Sheets or maintain a
   competing authoritative posting database.

4. React:
   Presentation and user interaction only. No trusted role, actor identity,
   target sheet, ID or business posting authority may originate from the UI.

5. MongoDB:
   Must not be a competing or sole authoritative business store. Any permitted
   technical cache/session use must be non-authoritative and reconstructible.

6. Reads and writes:
   Separate read/query routes from write commands. A read route must not mutate
   business records as a side effect.

7. One ordinary business posting boundary:
   All ordinary business mutations converge on one central Apps Script posting
   owner. Domain engines validate and prepare commands; they do not directly
   mutate canonical business tables.

8. Administrative operations:
   Destructive reset, cleanup, migration and cutover operations are outside
   ordinary business posting. They require a separate owner-only administrative
   path and separate operation authorization.

No schema, tab, header, ID, formula, ledger or historical data change is included
in this architecture decision.

3. O-02: TRUSTED GATEWAY AUTHENTICATION AND SIGNING
STATUS: RESOLVED DESIGN; SECRET PROVISIONING REQUIRES SEPARATE AUTHORIZATION
======================================================================

Adopt a gateway-to-Apps-Script signed request contract.

A. User authentication
- FastAPI authenticates the human user using the ERP's approved authentication
  mechanism.
- FastAPI resolves the user's active status and permissions from the approved
  user/role source.
- The browser cannot assert its own trusted actor ID, role or permission.
- The Apps Script bridge does not trust actor_user_id, actor_role, created_by,
  role or permission fields supplied in the business payload.

B. Gateway assertion
FastAPI forwards a signed, time-bound assertion containing:
- contract_version
- request_id
- action
- idempotency_key
- issued_at and expiry
- verified actor identifier
- authenticated gateway/service identity
- normalized payload
- payload digest
- signature

The signature must cover a deterministic canonical representation of all
security-relevant fields, including method, action, request ID, timestamp,
nonce/idempotency key, actor scope and payload digest.

C. Apps Script verification
- Missing, blank, malformed, invalid, expired or replayed signatures reject.
- Authentication occurs before any write-capable handler or sheet lookup that
  can lead to mutation.
- Use constant-time signature comparison.
- Enforce a short, documented clock-skew window.
- Replay identity is stored durably and bound to actor scope, action, target,
  payload digest and request identity.
- Unknown contract versions reject.

D. Signing algorithm and secret handling
- Use HMAC-SHA-256 for the initial gateway assertion contract.
- Keep the secret only in FastAPI's protected environment/secret configuration
  and Apps Script Script Properties; never in React, source files, logs, reports,
  URLs or ordinary payloads.
- Restrict Apps Script project editor access because project editors may be
  able to access script configuration and source.
- Secret creation, rotation and Script Properties changes are not authorized
  by this design document. They require a separate credential-management step.
- Do not reuse a user password or an unrelated platform credential as the HMAC
  secret.
- Define rotation with an overlap window and key version identifier before
  implementation.

E. Failure behavior
Every authentication or authorization failure rejects before mutation.
Do not fall back to unsigned requests, anonymous privileged writes or caller-
supplied roles.

4. O-03: ACTION REGISTRY AND ROLE MATRIX
STATUS: DESIGN RESOLVED; FINAL ACTION ENUMERATION PENDING SOURCE INVENTORY
======================================================================

A. Closed action registry
- Every write action must have an explicit immutable registry entry.
- Each entry defines: action key, domain handler, fixed target table(s), allowed
  fields, validation schema, required permission, ID policy, idempotency policy,
  related-record transaction contract and audit requirement.
- The caller may supply an action and business payload only.
- The caller cannot supply a sheet/tab/table destination or arbitrary field map.
- Unknown, disabled, retired or unregistered actions reject by default.
- No generic target-sheet writer or unknown-action fallback is allowed.

B. Registry construction
- Gemini must derive the proposed registry from all actual UI callbacks,
  FastAPI routes, Apps Script global functions and the 95 mutation-bearing
  functions identified by the E2D audit.
- Include indirect/dynamic sidebar calls and all reachable writer call chains.
- Map every action to the existing approved workbook table/header contract.
- Do not invent table names, columns, fields, role names or business workflows.
- Where mapping is unclear, mark NEEDS OWNER DECISION or NEEDS WORKBOOK MAPPING.
- No action becomes enabled merely because it exists in source code.

C. Role/permission model
- Use the approved Users and role/permission records as the source of active
  user identity and role assignment.
- Use permission keys per action, not client-supplied role labels.
- Enforce authorization in FastAPI and independently at the Apps Script
  governed-write boundary.
- Every externally callable Apps Script function must either enforce its own
  server-side guard or be made internal and reachable only through the guarded
  dispatcher.
- Menu visibility and hidden buttons are not security controls.
- Do not invent new roles or assign permissions to named employees.
- Preserve the existing approved role matrix. If the workbook/policy does not
  define a permission for an action, that action remains disabled until Owner
  approves the permission.

D. Suggested permission shape
Use permission identifiers such as domain.action, for example sales.create or
inventory.receipt, only after matching them to existing policy and workflows.
These are naming examples, not approved permissions.

5. O-04: LEGACY, MIGRATION, RESET AND MAINTENANCE FUNCTIONS
STATUS: RESOLVED CLASSIFICATION RULE; FUNCTION-BY-FUNCTION DECISIONS PENDING
======================================================================

Classify each function into exactly one category:

1. KEEP AS READ-ONLY:
   No mutation, no hidden side effect, authorization appropriate to data access.

2. ROUTE THROUGH CENTRAL POSTING OWNER:
   Ordinary business transaction mutation.

3. INTERNALIZE:
   Helper may remain but must not be externally callable or independently
   reachable as an unguarded global business operation.

4. OWNER-ONLY ADMIN:
   Destructive, migration, reset, cleanup, cutover, schema or repair capability.
   Isolated from public bridge and ordinary sidebar. Requires an explicit
   operation ID, exact target workbook identity, target allowlist, preflight
   fingerprint, dry-run report, separate commit authorization, expiry,
   idempotency and audit evidence.

5. RETIRE AFTER OWNER APPROVAL:
   Duplicate or obsolete function with no approved operational use. Do not
   delete it automatically during the security repair.

6. DISABLE FROM PUBLIC ROUTE:
   Function may remain for controlled editor/admin use but cannot be called
   through public web-app action dispatch.

Rules:
- No automatic reset, cleanup, migration, deletion or historical restoration.
- Do not run any destructive function during this mission.
- Do not remove legacy functions until call sites, dependencies and operational
  purpose are documented and Owner approves retirement.
- All 95 write-bearing entries and all public/global callable functions must
  receive a classification and call-chain evidence.

6. O-05: REPORT AND OUTPUT WRITES
STATUS: RESOLVED DESIGN
======================================================================

Use a separate governed reporting-output writer, not the canonical business
transaction posting owner.

Rules:
- Reporting writer is a distinct, narrowly scoped component.
- It may write only to an explicit allowlist of report/output tabs.
- It cannot write to canonical business tables, transaction ledgers, inventory
  movement, accounting journals, sales records, employee records or master data.
- It cannot allocate business IDs or alter source transactions.
- Report outputs must be reproducible from authoritative source records.
- Report writes require appropriate authorization and audit metadata.
- If a report is stored as a snapshot, identify it as a derived report, not a
  source transaction.
- Any output destination not proven to be a report/output tab remains disabled
  pending workbook mapping and Owner approval.

7. O-06: MANDATORY AUDIT FAILURE AND UNKNOWN RECOVERY
STATUS: RESOLVED DESIGN
======================================================================

A. CONFIRMED
Return CONFIRMED only when all required steps for the transaction are durable
and verified:
- business record(s) written;
- required related records/ledgers written;
- canonical ID allocation verified;
- idempotency/transaction record verified;
- mandatory audit evidence verified;
- complete readback of IDs, values and relationship keys verified.

B. Audit failure
- If the business write has not started, reject/fail safely.
- If the business write may have occurred but mandatory audit evidence is
  missing or uncertain, return UNKNOWN / RECOVERY_REQUIRED.
- Do not claim FAILED if the transaction may have committed.
- Do not claim CONFIRMED without mandatory audit evidence.
- Do not blindly retry by creating a new transaction.

C. Recovery
- Recovery uses the exact original request identity and idempotency key.
- Reconcile business rows, related ledgers, allocation, audit and readback.
- If all required durable evidence is found and consistent, mark the original
  request CONFIRMED.
- If no business mutation occurred and this is proven, mark REJECTED/FAILED
  according to the response contract.
- If state remains partial or ambiguous, keep UNKNOWN / RECOVERY_REQUIRED and
  route to an authorized reconciliation queue.
- Never create a duplicate transaction to resolve uncertainty.

D. Reversal
- Do not silently edit or delete a committed transaction to repair it.
- Use approved reversal/correction transactions where the business/accounting
  policy requires them.
- Exact accounting treatment, approval thresholds and journal mappings remain
  governed by Owner/CA-approved accounting policy.

E. Response states
Use a versioned response contract with at least:
CONFIRMED, REJECTED, CONFLICT, UNKNOWN, RECOVERY_REQUIRED.
Do not expose internal secrets or sensitive employee data in responses.

8. O-07: DEPLOYMENT ACCESS MODEL
STATUS: RESOLVED TARGET; CURRENT DEPLOYMENT FACTS PENDING EVIDENCE
======================================================================

Target:
- No anonymous/public access to a privileged write endpoint.
- Authenticated gateway-only access for write requests.
- Least privilege for service identities and human project editors.
- No deployment or OAuth scope change during design or patch preparation.
- Read-only endpoints, if any, must have their own explicit access policy and
  must not share a privileged anonymous write route.

Before any deployment decision, collect evidence for:
- active deployment ID and version;
- effective URL and access setting;
- execution identity;
- deployment owner;
- source version/hash associated with the deployment;
- OAuth scopes;
- service account/editor access;
- evidence date and collector.

Reconcile the previously observed difference between the Overview status
"Deployed" and Deployment Manager showing no active or archived deployment.
Neither screenshot alone proves the effective deployed write endpoint.

If effective access cannot be proven, deployment status remains UNKNOWN and
production deployment is blocked. Do not change access settings to make the
evidence appear consistent.

10. BUSINESS POLICY ITEMS THAT MUST NOT BE INVENTED
======================================================================

These are not technical design choices and cannot responsibly be finalized by
an AI from the available documents alone:

1. Accounting treatment, chart of accounts, tax treatment and journal mapping:
   CA/professional verification required where the policy source says so.

2. Financial approval thresholds, payment limits, discount limits, credit limits,
   write-off limits and exception authority:
   Use existing approved policy only. If absent, the action stays disabled until
   Owner approves a threshold.

3. Employee-specific roles, permissions, salary, incentive, disciplinary or
   confidential HR decisions:
   Do not infer or disclose. Use the approved confidential policy and authorized
   Owner decisions only.

4. Statutory, tax, FSSAI, labour and other legal/compliance interpretations:
   Verify against applicable law and qualified professional advice. Do not
   encode an assumption as a system rule.

5. Exact action-to-table mapping and field allowlists:
   Must be derived from the approved workbook dictionary and physical headers,
   not invented from function names or UI labels.

6. Whether a legacy feature is still operationally required:
   Requires dependency evidence and Owner decision before retirement.

Safe default for every unresolved policy:
DISABLED / BLOCKED, not guessed and not silently permitted.
