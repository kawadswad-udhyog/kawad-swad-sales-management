1. AUTHORITY, SOURCE HIERARCHY, AND INTERPRETATION
====================================================================

1.1 Authority hierarchy

When sources conflict, apply this order:

1. Applicable law and binding government directions, verified for Kawad Swad's actual entity, activities, premises, and circumstances.
2. This Constitution after Owner approval.
3. Owner-approved amendments and decision records, provided they do not contradict law.
4. The approved Master Database & Business Policy Blueprint and its approved Data Dictionary/Table Register.
5. CA-approved accounting policy, chart of accounts, tax treatment, and approval matrix.
6. Approved workbook schema and versioned Apps Script release.
7. Verified source-code behavior.
8. Implementation reports, developer notes, AI summaries, and informal instructions.
9. Assumptions, which must never be treated as approved policy.

A report or developer statement does not prove implementation. Source code is evidence of implementation, but source code does not override approved policy. Runtime verification is required when the acceptance criterion demands it.

1.2 Source of truth

Google Sheets is the authoritative business database for core business records. It is the canonical source for master data, operational transactions, inventory movement, production, sales, workforce, financial and accounting records, controls, and business audit information.

Apps Script is the approved governed-write and automation layer for business validations, controlled writes, ID allocation, locking, idempotency, workflow transitions, calculations, posting, and reporting automation.

Reports, dashboards, caches, exports, backups, frontend state, and external services are secondary representations. They must be traceable or reconcilable to authoritative records and must not become competing business databases.

1.3 Project scope

This Constitution governs the ERP upgrade and completion program, including:
- Business requirements and policies.
- Database design and governance.
- Backend, API, frontend, Apps Script and integration behavior.
- Operational workflows and calculations.
- Security and authorization.
- Reporting and reconciliation.
- Verification, release, cutover, and maintenance.

1.4 No implied implementation authorization

This document does not authorize, by itself:
- Live Google Sheets modification.
- Production business-data creation, editing, deletion, restoration, or migration.
- ID allocation or reservation in production.
- Apps Script deployment or execution against live business records.
- GitHub pushes or changes to protected branches.
- Render/cloud deployment or configuration changes.
- Credential, token, or secret rotation.
- Historical pre-reset data restoration.
- Testing, builds, linting, typechecking, runtime checks, or production verification unless separately authorized.

1.5 Requirement status definitions

NOT STARTED: Work has not begun.
PLANNED: Task and intended outcome are defined.
IN PROGRESS: Work is underway.
IMPLEMENTED: Code or configuration is present; not yet verified.
VERIFICATION PENDING: Verification is required but not performed.
VERIFIED: Evidence meets the specified verification level.
ACCEPTED: Owner or designated acceptance authority approves it.
BLOCKED: A dependency prevents progress.
DEFERRED: Owner explicitly postpones it.
FAILED: Verification found an unacceptable defect.

Never collapse IMPLEMENTED, VERIFIED, and ACCEPTED into one status.

2. CONSTITUTIONAL PRINCIPLES (NON-NEGOTIABLE)
====================================================================

POL-001 — Google Sheets Authority
Core business records reside in the approved Google Sheets database. Secondary copies must be reconcilable. Routine business entry must not occur in a shadow database.

POL-002 — Permanent Identity
Each record receives a unique, immutable, non-recycled ID from the approved ID allocation policy. Row numbers, names, dates, descriptions, or phone numbers are not primary keys.

POL-003 — Fail Clearly, Never Pretend
A transaction cannot be reported as successful unless mandatory validation, write, and required verification succeeded. Missing headers, invalid references, failed writes, timeouts, and uncertain outcomes must be explicit.

POL-004 — Posted History Is Append-Only
Posted transactions, journal entries, receipts, payments, stock movements, production records, and other material history must not be silently overwritten or deleted. Corrections use linked reversals, adjustments, returns, credit/debit notes, or approved correction records.

POL-005 — Movement-Led Inventory
Stock balance is derived from stock movements and valid opening balances. Direct balance editing is prohibited. Every change must have an identifiable event, item/lot, quantity, unit, location, source, actor, timestamp, and reason where applicable.

POL-006 — Every Money Event Is Recorded
Money received, paid, earned, owed, advanced, refunded, transferred, or adjusted must have a record, source reference, account treatment, and evidence where applicable.

POL-007 — Physical, Commercial, and Financial Events Are Distinct
Purchase order is not receipt; receipt is not supplier bill; bill is not payment. Sales order, invoice, receipt, dispatch, delivery, return, settlement, and bank confirmation are distinct events linked by IDs.

POL-008 — Double-Entry Control
Transactions requiring accounting posting must create balanced debit and credit lines. Direct editing of account balances is prohibited.

POL-009 — Traceable Source Documents
Invoices, receipts, bills, approvals, bank references, quality checks, delivery evidence, and other documents must link to the originating record. An attachment alone does not prove a transaction.

POL-010 — Effective-Dated Master Data
Recipes, prices, tax settings, labels, credit terms, limits, and other changeable master data retain effective dates and versions. Transactions preserve the applicable snapshot.

POL-011 — Segregation of Duties
Where practicable, request, approve, execute/pay, custody, reconcile, and quality-release duties are separated. Exceptions require recorded rationale, authority, and review.

POL-012 — Least Privilege and Confidentiality
Access is limited to role-required information. Salary, personal identity, bank, employee documents, confidential policies, and sensitive finance records are restricted. Sensitive data must not leak into general reports or logs.

POL-013 — No Fabricated Records
Attendance, production, inspection, receipts, payments, approvals, delivery, and filing evidence must never be invented. Unknown values remain UNKNOWN, PENDING, or unresolved with an explanation.

POL-014 — Reconciliation Is a Control
Cash, bank, stock, parties, marketplace settlement, taxes, and control accounts must reconcile on defined schedules. Differences are recorded, assigned, and resolved with evidence.

POL-015 — Plain Operational Language
Factory, store, packaging, and sales forms use understandable language. Technical and accounting terminology may be used in professional reports with explanations.

POL-016 — Unapproved Assumptions Are Not Policy
Tax, valuation, costing, wages, credit, approval limits, legal applicability, and thresholds require Owner and/or professional approval. No developer or AI may silently hard-code an assumption.

POL-017 — Clean Baseline Boundary
The clean post-reset database is the active baseline. Pre-reset records and backups are preservation/reference evidence only. Do not restore, import, regenerate, or reserve historical business IDs unless separately authorized.

POL-018 — Controlled Change
Live workbook, schema, formulas, validations, scripts, and policies must be changed through reviewed change request, impact assessment, backup, disposable-copy verification, rollback plan, and explicit Owner release authorization.

POL-019 — Owner Commercial Authority
Owner controls product identity, prices, discounts, credit, write-offs, spending limits, stock adjustments, role assignments, and reserved commercial decisions.

POL-020 — Law and Professional Advice
Legal, GST, FSSAI, packaged-commodity, labour, and other compliance applicability must be confirmed by qualified professionals where needed. ERP supports evidence and workflows; it does not replace professional determinations.

POL-021 — Single Governed Write Path
Business mutations must use the approved governed-write path. Frontend and FastAPI must not independently mutate business records or bypass Apps Script controls.

POL-022 — Fail-Closed Security
Missing configuration, unknown roles, invalid authorization, schema uncertainty, invalid signatures, missing response echoes, or unverified transaction state must fail closed. No permissive fallback.

POL-023 — Idempotent Mutations
Retrying the same logical submission with the same idempotency key must not create duplicate business effects. A nonce cannot be silently replaced during retry of an uncertain transaction.

POL-024 — No Destructive Read Operations
Reads, startup discovery, and report refresh must not silently create tabs, alter headers, insert business records, allocate IDs, or mutate authoritative data.

POL-025 — No Fake or Silent Fallback Data
The application must not quietly switch to local demo data, browser persistence, synthetic business records, or fallback databases in production. If authoritative data is unavailable, show the state honestly.

POL-026 — Visible Exception Management
Important failures, discrepancies, missing evidence, and unresolved transactions must be visible, assigned, and trackable. Do not bury errors in logs alone.

POL-027 — No Scope Drift
Implementation must be checked against approved requirements. New features, architecture changes, or policy changes require a change record and Owner approval.

POL-028 — Completion Requires Evidence
An AI or developer claim is not acceptance evidence. Evidence level must be recorded: source inspection, static checks, test evidence, runtime evidence, live integration evidence, or Owner acceptance, as applicable.

3. SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE POLICY
====================================================================

3.1 Target architecture

AUTHORITATIVE BUSINESS DATABASE:
Google Sheets, approved workbook and schema.

GOVERNED BUSINESS-WRITE/AUTOMATION OWNER:
Google Apps Script. Responsible for validation, authorization controls where applicable, IDs, locking, idempotency, workflow controls, posting, governed mutations, and verified outcomes.

BACKEND:
FastAPI. Responsible for authentication/session handling, API gateway, RBAC enforcement, request validation, read/query services, and forwarding governed business mutations to Apps Script. It must not be an alternate business-write owner.

FRONTEND:
React + TypeScript + Vite (as the current project baseline). Responsible for UI, user workflows, input validation assistance, clear statuses, and safe submission handling. It is not authoritative for business rules or balances.

REPORTING:
Derived from authoritative source records. Reports are views and must reconcile to underlying tables. Snapshot reports are stored only where approved closing or audit requirements justify them.

3.2 Prohibited competing stores
No MongoDB, SQLite, PostgreSQL, browser localStorage/IndexedDB, or other system may become a competing authoritative business database. Temporary technical state may exist only if explicitly approved, non-authoritative, safe, and never used as a silent business-data fallback.

3.3 Frontend standards
- Preserve established visual design unless redesign is approved.
- Responsive desktop/tablet/mobile layouts.
- Clear loading, empty, success, failure, and unknown-outcome states.
- Avoid native browser alert/confirm/prompt for critical workflows; use accessible in-app dialogs.
- Prevent duplicate submissions while requests are in flight.
- Preserve idempotency keys through retry and uncertainty handling.
- No raw fetch bypass of the centralized API client except approved technical exceptions.
- Never claim success from optimistic UI alone.

3.4 Backend standards
- Typed request/response contracts.
- Centralized authentication and authorization.
- Explicit role checks at route and action level.
- Input validation and normalization.
- Controlled business mutation delegation.
- Consistent error mapping.
- Request correlation.
- Safe timeouts and unknown-outcome handling.
- No direct business-table mutation outside the approved Apps Script bridge.
- No secrets in source code, logs, responses, or client bundles.

3.5 Apps Script standards
- Governed action allowlist.
- Canonical target table per action.
- Controlled ID allocation.
- Locking for concurrency-sensitive operations.
- Idempotency and duplicate protection.
- Required field and business validation.
- Mandatory read-back for critical transactions.
- Response echo of correlation ID, nonce, action, target table, and transaction result where required.
- Append-only posting behavior.
- Audit record.
- Clear, structured failure.
- No uncontrolled tab/header auto-creation during reads/startup.
- Explicit safeguards against partial posting.

4. BUSINESS PROCESS MASTER
====================================================================

Each business process must be documented with:
Process ID; purpose; owner role; initiating event; prerequisites; inputs; validation; workflow states; approvals; authoritative writes; stock/financial effects; source documents; notifications; exceptions; reversals; reports; permissions; acceptance criteria.

4.1 Master Data
Owner-approved setup for business identity, sites/locations, warehouses, products, variants, SKUs, units, recipes, suppliers, customers, channels, price lists, payment modes, expense categories, chart of accounts, users, roles, approval limits, tax setup, and other reference data.

4.2 Procurement-to-Stock
Requirement -> request/quotation as applicable -> approval -> purchase order -> goods receipt -> quantity/quality inspection -> accepted/rejected/quarantined quantities -> supplier bill -> payable -> payment -> allocation -> supplier reconciliation.

4.3 Manufacturing
Factory opening -> plan/order -> approved recipe version -> raw-material lot selection -> issue/consumption -> milling/preparation -> production batch -> actual output -> process loss/recovery -> quality check -> hold/release/reject -> cost calculation -> bulk stock.

4.4 Packaging
Packaging plan -> released bulk lot selection -> packaging material issue -> packaging batch -> packet count and weight validation -> damages/loss -> approved label version -> QC release -> finished SKU lot -> finished-goods inventory.

4.5 Sales-to-Cash
Enquiry/order -> customer/channel -> price/tax/discount validation -> payment or credit authorization -> stock reservation -> pick/pack -> invoice -> dispatch -> delivery -> return/claim where applicable -> receipt/marketplace settlement -> allocation -> bank/cash reconciliation -> close.

4.6 Workforce-to-Payroll
Employee/worker master -> approved role and pay basis -> attendance/shift -> work assignment/output -> verification -> leave/overtime/incentive/advance rules -> payroll calculation -> approval -> payment -> evidence -> reconciliation.

4.7 Expense-to-Payment
Request/bill -> category and cost centre -> evidence -> approval -> voucher -> payment -> accounting posting -> tax treatment where applicable -> review.

4.8 Financial Close
Complete source records -> post all valid transactions -> reconcile cash/bank/parties/stock/tax/control accounts -> resolve or disclose exceptions -> trial balance -> approved adjustments -> statements -> close and lock period -> archive.

4.9 Transaction states
Use only defined, controlled states. General lifecycle may be:
DRAFT -> SUBMITTED -> APPROVED -> POSTED -> RECONCILED -> CLOSED.
Contextual states may include REJECTED, CANCELLED, ON_HOLD, PARTIALLY_RECEIVED, PARTIALLY_PAID, PARTIALLY_DISPATCHED, RETURNED, REVERSED.
Each transition must define role, evidence, validation, side effects, and allowed next states. A canceled or rejected record retains its ID and history.

5. DATABASE DESIGN AND GOVERNANCE
====================================================================

5.1 Logical table layers
A. Master tables.
B. Transaction header tables.
C. Transaction detail/line tables.
D. Movement and subledger tables.
E. Control tables: IDs, approvals, exceptions, audit, status history, configuration.
F. Document register linking evidence.
G. Reporting views, which are derived and not original transactions.

5.2 Table contract
Every physical table must have a documented contract:
- Canonical table ID and exact tab name.
- Purpose and record grain.
- Owner department and accountable role.
- Primary key and unique constraints.
- Exact headers and types.
- Required/optional fields.
- Foreign keys and relationships.
- Valid values and status lifecycle.
- Read/write permissions.
- Sensitivity classification.
- Posting/movement rules.
- Source and downstream tables.
- Reports consuming the table.
- Retention rules.
- Apps Script functions/actions using it.
- Implementation status and evidence.

5.3 Common fields
Where appropriate, records include:
record_id or domain-specific immutable ID;
created_at;
created_by;
updated_at/updated_by only for allowed pre-posting edits;
status;
source/reference;
notes/reason;
approval metadata;
effective date/version;
correlation/idempotency metadata where needed.
Use fields appropriate to the record grain. Do not force irrelevant common fields into every table.

5.4 Data type and validation
Define types, units, precision, requiredness, allowable values, date/time conventions, text limits, uniqueness, and foreign-key rules. Invalid or missing data must not be silently coerced to fabricated values.

5.5 Relationships
Use stable IDs for all relationships. Names and descriptions are display values, not linkage keys. Validate referenced records and status before posting.

5.6 One-to-many
Use separate line/detail tables for multi-line purchases, production materials, invoice items, payments allocations, journal lines, packaging consumption, and similar relationships. Do not store repeated line items as ambiguous delimited strings.

5.7 Derived balances
Stock, cash, bank, receivable, payable, worker payable, and account balances are derived from validated movements, postings, and approved opening balances. Balances must be traceable and reconcileable; no independent manual balance field may override the ledger.

5.8 Master-data versioning
Changing recipes, prices, tax setup, labels, approval limits, or other effective-dated rules creates a version/effective-date history. Posted transactions retain the snapshot that applied.

5.9 Workbook layout
Tabs are classified as source tables, control tables, reference tables, derived reports, or technical metadata. Logical capabilities do not automatically require a separate physical tab. Do not create tabs solely for reports unless the report or control requirement is approved.

5.10 Clean baseline
Do not restore or import pre-reset transactions. Opening balances, if later approved, must be prepared from independently verified sources, have a cut-off date, approval, and reconciliation. Historic backups are preservation/reference only.

6. IDENTITY, NONCE, AND TRANSACTION SAFETY
====================================================================

6.1 ID policy
- Approved ID prefixes and formats are centrally controlled.
- IDs are immutable and never recycled.
- IDs are not derived from row number, user-entered name, phone, date, or client guess.
- Allocation must be concurrency-safe and auditable.
- Cancelled IDs remain consumed.
- Failed allocations must not be silently reused if policy marks them consumed.

6.2 Idempotency
Each governed write uses a stable submission nonce/idempotency key. A retry of the same logical transaction uses the same key. If the result is uncertain, the client must not invent a new nonce and submit a second transaction. The system must provide a recovery/reconciliation path.

6.3 Unknown outcome
When a request times out after submission or the response is ambiguous:
- Do not show success.
- Do not automatically create a new transaction.
- Show “Outcome unknown / checking transaction status.”
- Retain nonce and correlation ID.
- Allow safe status recovery or Owner-authorized reconciliation.
- Do not invite unsafe duplicate resubmission.

6.4 Response envelope
Critical governed-write responses must include and validate the required request/transaction echoes, including correlation ID, nonce, action, target table, status, and allocated ID or record identity as applicable. Missing or mismatched required echoes fail closed.

7. CALCULATION AND FORMULA GOVERNANCE
====================================================================

7.1 Calculation rule
Every business calculation must have a Calculation Register entry containing:
Calculation ID; name; purpose; formula; input fields; unit; precision; rounding; sample calculation; edge cases; validation; posting impact; report usage; policy owner; approval status; effective version.

No formula may be implemented solely as undocumented code.

7.2 Units and conversions
Approved base units and conversions must be centrally defined. Examples:
1 kg = 1,000 g.
1 litre = 1,000 ml.
Packet count, carton count, and unit count require explicit pack configuration.
Conversions must not be applied between incompatible units. Precision and rounding are defined per calculation, not guessed.

7.3 Quantity calculations
- Line quantity = entered quantity in the permitted unit.
- Normalized quantity = entered quantity × approved conversion factor.
- Received quantity, accepted quantity, rejected quantity, and quarantined quantity must be separate where applicable.
- Actual balance = validated opening balance + valid incoming movements − valid outgoing movements, with signed movements and transfers handled consistently.
- Available quantity = physical/ledger quantity − reserved quantity − blocked quantity, according to approved reservation policy.

7.4 Purchase calculations
For each purchase line:
Line Gross = Quantity × Unit Rate.
Line Discount = policy-defined discount calculation.
Taxable Base = Gross − applicable discount + included/excluded charges as defined by approved tax policy.
Tax = taxable base × approved tax rate (or documented tax calculation method).
Line Total = taxable base + tax + allocated charges.
Document Total = sum of valid line totals + document-level charges − document-level discounts, without double-counting tax or freight.
Tolerance for quantity/rate differences must be Owner-approved.

7.5 Production calculations
- Planned material requirement = approved recipe quantity × planned batch scale factor.
- Actual material consumption = sum of validated material issues/returns for the batch.
- Net input = total issued quantity − valid returns/reversals.
- Net output = accepted output according to the approved output definition.
- Yield % = Net Output ÷ Net Input × 100, only when denominator > 0 and units are compatible.
- Loss Quantity = input quantity − output quantity only where the process definition supports direct comparison and recoverable/by-product streams are separately accounted.
- Loss % = Loss Quantity ÷ Input Quantity × 100, only for positive denominator.
- Recovery/rework must be recorded separately; it must not silently reduce loss.
- Recipe theoretical consumption, actual consumption, variance, and variance % are separate metrics.
- No negative consumption or output unless an approved correction/reversal workflow supports it.

7.6 Production costing
Batch cost includes only approved cost components and allocation basis:
raw material cost;
direct labour;
manufacturing overhead;
approved utilities;
other directly attributable cost;
normal/abnormal loss treatment as approved.
Actual ingredient cost uses approved source price and valuation policy. Cost per unit = approved total batch cost ÷ approved output quantity. The denominator, treatment of rejected output, WIP, loss, and rounding must be approved before automated posting.

7.7 Packaging calculations
- Theoretical packets = available bulk quantity converted to the SKU unit ÷ net weight per packet.
- Good packets, damaged packets, samples, and rejected packets are separate counts.
- Bulk consumption = net packet weight × packets produced + approved process/packaging loss, normalized to base unit.
- Pack reconciliation = bulk issued − bulk consumed in good packs − damaged/rejected product consumption − documented loss − returned remainder.
- Pack-material usage = actual verified packaging components issued/consumed, not theoretical assumptions alone.
- SKU lot quantity = verified good packets accepted by required QC.
- Packaging discrepancy must be visible and dispositioned.

7.8 Inventory calculations
- Closing quantity = opening quantity + valid receipts + valid production output + returns/transfer-in + approved adjustments-in − issues/consumption − dispatch/transfer-out − damages/expiry/adjustments-out.
- Movement ledger, not editable balance cells, is the basis.
- Reserved stock is distinct from physical stock.
- Available stock = eligible physical stock − reservations − quality hold/blocked stock, as applicable.
- Lot-level balances must be calculated and traceable.
- Inventory valuation formula and cost layers require Owner/CA approval before automation.
- Negative stock/overdraw must be blocked unless a separately approved policy exception exists.

7.9 Sales calculations
- Line gross = quantity × unit selling price.
- Line discount = approved discount method.
- Tax base and tax = approved invoice/tax rules.
- Invoice total = sum of line totals + charges − discounts, avoiding double counting.
- Amount received = sum of verified receipt allocations.
- Outstanding = valid invoice amount − allocated receipts/credits/refunds, with advances and unallocated receipts separately represented.
- Return quantity/value must link to original sale/invoice and disposition.
- Sales revenue, tax collected, receipt, settlement, fees, and bank credit are separate events.
- Net sales/profit metrics must state their tax and cost basis.

7.10 Payment and allocation calculations
- Payment amount is recorded once.
- Allocations cannot exceed payment amount or outstanding document balance except approved overpayment/advance treatment.
- Unallocated remainder stays visible as unallocated receipt/advance.
- Payment reversal/refund is linked, not erased.
- Split payments retain separate payment references and allocation history.

7.11 Workforce and payroll calculations
Pay calculations must follow an approved wage basis and rule version.
- Attendance/shift quantities are verified before payable calculation.
- Piece-rate payable = verified eligible output × approved rate, adjusted only by approved deductions/bonuses.
- Daily/shift payable = verified payable days/shifts × approved rate.
- Fixed salary payable = approved salary components + eligible overtime/incentives − approved advance recovery/deductions.
- Advances remain separate transactions and are allocated/recovered explicitly.
- Gig worker entries must pass SUBMITTED -> VERIFIED -> PAID (or approved exception path).
- Fixed employee payroll must be calculated for a defined period and linked to payment evidence.
- Confidential payroll fields are restricted.
Exact rates, attendance rules, overtime, incentives, deductions, and legal treatment remain pending Owner/professional approval unless already explicitly approved.

7.12 Expenses
Expense total = sum of valid expense lines plus applicable charges/tax less approved discounts/credits. Paid and unpaid status are separate from expense recognition. Personal and business costs are not mixed. Reimbursements require evidence and approval.

7.13 Accounting calculations
- Journal debits must equal journal credits per voucher before posting.
- Trial balance total debits must equal total credits.
- Ledger balance = opening balance + posted debit/credit activity using account normal-balance convention.
- Receivable/payable subledgers reconcile to control accounts.
- Cash/bank book balances reconcile to opening plus valid receipts/transfers minus valid payments/transfers.
- Transfers between cash/bank accounts are not income or expense.
- Customer receipt allocated to invoice reduces receivable; it is not revenue a second time.
- Supplier payment allocated to bill reduces payable; it is not purchase expense a second time.
- Profit measures must be distinguished: gross profit, contribution, operating profit, net profit. Reports label actual, standard, estimated, or allocated basis.
- Tax and recognition mapping are CA-confirmed.

7.14 Rounding and precision
- Money in governed Apps Script calculations should use integer paise where the current architecture specifies; backend calculations should use Decimal, not binary floating-point.
- Conversion between rupees and paise must be explicit.
- Quantity precision depends on approved unit and process.
- Rounding mode, intermediate precision, and final rounding point must be specified for each calculation.
- Never round each intermediate component if policy requires rounding only at final line/document level.
- The exact rounding method is a pending policy decision unless explicitly approved.

7.15 Calculation edge cases
Every calculation must document zero denominator, negative quantity, partial receipt, partial payment, returns, cancellation, tax rounding, discount order, freight allocation, missing rate, missing cost, unit conversion, precision overflow, and duplicate submission behavior where relevant.

8. INVENTORY, LOT, QUALITY, AND TRACEABILITY POLICY
====================================================================

8.1 Inventory categories
Track raw material, packaging material, WIP, bulk finished product, packaged finished goods, samples, rejected/held stock, and any other approved stock class distinctly.

8.2 Lot traceability
A lot must link item/material, supplier or source, receipt/production event, quantity, unit, location, quality status, relevant date fields, and downstream uses. Lot identity is stable.

8.3 Quality status
Quality lifecycle and allowed status transitions are defined for each applicable class. The system blocks consumption, packaging, or dispatch of held/rejected/unreleased lots where policy requires release.

8.4 Movements
Receipts, issues, transfers, production consumption/output, packaging conversion, dispatch, returns, damage, expiry, loss, adjustments, and reversals are separate movement events with references.

8.5 Reconciliation
Physical counts compare with ledger-derived quantity. Discrepancy requires reason, evidence, approval, and adjustment movement. Never overwrite balance to force agreement.

8.6 Recall/trace
The system must support tracing a finished SKU lot back through packaging batch, bulk lot, production batch, ingredient/raw material lots, and supplier/receipt records; and tracing source lots forward to affected production, packaging, sales, and dispatch records.

====================================================================
9. SALES, CHANNELS, CUSTOMER, AND DISTRIBUTION POLICY
====================================================================

9.1 Channel identity
Each order identifies the customer or channel, channel order reference where available, source, price policy/version, payment/credit terms, and fulfillment path.

9.2 Sales events
Order, reservation, pick/pack, invoice, dispatch, delivery, return, refund, payment receipt, channel settlement, fees, and bank receipt are distinct linked events.

9.3 Pricing authority
Owner-approved price lists, discounts, channel fees, credit limits, and overrides. Historical sales preserve price/discount/tax snapshots.

9.4 Credit
Credit sales require approved credit terms and limit checks. Exposure calculation and exception authority are defined. Credit policy must not be invented by code.

9.5 Returns
Returns link to original sale, invoice, SKU/lot, quantity, reason, inspection, disposition, credit/refund, stock movement, and accounting treatment.

9.6 Marketplace settlement
Gross order value, platform deductions, fees, refunds, withholding/tax deductions where applicable, settlement amount, settlement reference, and bank credit are reconciled as separate values. Marketplace “paid” or “delivered” status alone is not proof of bank settlement.

9.7 Dispatch and delivery
Dispatch requires eligible released stock, order/invoice checks, quantities, carrier/vehicle/courier details as applicable, dispatch proof, and delivery status/evidence. Dispatch is not automatically delivery.

====================================================================
10. FINANCE AND ACCOUNTING POLICY
====================================================================

10.1 Chart of Accounts
Before routine journal automation, approve account code, name, class, parent/group, normal balance, control-account flag, linked subledger, manual-posting permissions, reporting/tax mapping, effective dates, and history.

Suggested groups (illustrative only, not a final chart):
ASSETS: cash, bank, receivables, supplier advances, raw/WIP/finished inventory, eligible tax credits, prepaid expenses, fixed assets, accumulated depreciation, deposits/other assets.
LIABILITIES: trade payables, customer advances, tax payables, wages payable, expenses payable, loans and other liabilities.
EQUITY: owner capital, drawings, retained earnings/accumulated profit, current-period result as approved.
INCOME: product sales, other operating income, sales returns/allowances as contra-income.
COST OF SALES: raw materials consumed, direct labour, manufacturing overhead, packaging cost, inventory cost of goods sold, approved production loss.
EXPENSES: selling/marketplace fees, freight, advertising, admin, repairs, utilities, rent, professional fees, bank/payment fees, depreciation, interest, and approved others.

10.2 Opening balances
Opening balances require cut-off date, verified source, preparation/review/approval, and reconciliation. Categories may include cash, bank, document-level receivables/payables, advances, inventory by item/lot/location, assets, liabilities, loans, equity, and tax balances. No historic pre-reset data may be restored without separate Owner authorization.

10.3 Journal posting
A journal requires voucher identity, date, period, narration, source reference, lines, account, debit/credit, dimensions as used, supporting evidence, requester, approver, and posting state.
Posting requires:
- Debits equal credits.
- Active/postable accounts.
- Open financial period.
- Required evidence and approvals.
- No bypass of tax, party, stock, cash/bank controls.
- Successful governed write and required read-back.

10.4 Conceptual posting templates
Exact mappings require CA approval.
Cash sale: debit cash/bank/payment clearing; credit sales revenue and applicable tax components.
Credit sale: debit receivables; credit revenue and applicable tax.
Customer receipt: debit cash/bank/clearing; credit receivables. Unallocated excess is an advance/unallocated receipt, not duplicate revenue.
Inventory purchase: debit inventory/tax/appropriate account; credit payable according to approved recognition.
Supplier payment: debit payable or supplier advance; credit cash/bank.
Supplier advance: debit supplier advances; credit cash/bank.
Material issue: debit WIP/production cost; credit raw material inventory.
Production completion: debit bulk/finished inventory; credit WIP.
Packaging: transfer bulk inventory and approved conversion cost into packaged inventory.
Dispatch/sale: recognize COGS and reduce inventory at the CA-approved recognition point and valuation basis.
Expense: debit expense/prepayment/asset; credit cash/bank or payable.
Transfer: debit receiving cash/bank and credit sending account; not income/expense.
Return/refund: linked reversal/credit note, stock inspection/disposition, and tax/accounting adjustment.

10.5 Receivables/payables
Maintain document-level balances, due dates, terms, allocations, credits, disputes, advances, and ageing. Unallocated cash remains visible. Subledgers reconcile to control accounts.

10.6 Cash and bank
Cash custodian/location must be identified. Daily cash control includes opening, receipts, payments, deposits/withdrawals, physical count, expected close, actual close, and variance explanation. Bank entries match statement lines; unmatched items remain assigned and open.

10.7 Expense
Expense record contains date, payee, purpose, category, cost centre, amount, tax fields if applicable, payment mode, evidence, requester, approver, and accounting state. Personal expenses are not mixed. Owner drawings/reimbursements are distinct.

10.8 Profit reporting
Gross profit = net revenue less COGS on approved accounting basis.
Contribution = net selling value excluding applicable taxes collected for government, less directly attributable costs as defined by approved report policy.
Operating profit = gross profit/contribution measure less approved recorded/allocated operating expenses as report defines.
Net profit = after finance, depreciation, taxes, and other required items on approved basis.
Reports must label actual, standard, estimated, and allocated figures. Management contribution is not formal P&L.

10.9 Inventory valuation
Owner and CA approve method and cost-layer policy, freight/duties/tax treatment, normal/abnormal loss, by-products, WIP, finished goods, returns, and write-offs before automated valuation.

10.10 Periods
Define fiscal year, periods, posting cutoff, adjustment window, close review, statement approval, and opening. Closed period is read-only except controlled reopen/adjustment with Owner/CA approval and audit.

11. WORKFORCE, ATTENDANCE, AND PAYROLL

11.2 Attendance

12. ROLE, ACCESS, AND APPROVAL GOVERNANCE

12.1 Canonical roles

READ; CREATE; EDIT; SUBMIT; APPROVE; REJECT; CANCEL; REVERSE; EXPORT; ADMINISTER.
Row-level or scoped access (salesperson, location, assigned customers, etc.) must be explicitly defined.

12.5 Sensitive information

12.6 Authorization enforcement
Frontend hiding is not security. Permissions must be enforced server-side and at governed-action level. Unknown roles, missing identity, and failed authorization deny access.

13. UI/UX POLICY
====================================================================

13.1 Design consistency

13.2 Form behavior

14. SECURITY, AUDIT, AND DATA PRIVACY
====================================================================

14.1 Authentication
Use approved authentication mechanisms, safe token/session handling, logout/revocation, secure password handling, and account lifecycle. Authentication exceptions are limited to explicitly public health/liveness or login functions.

14.2 Authorization
Every sensitive route/action is guarded server-side. Sensitive reads and writes have explicit role/scoping rules. AUDITOR remains read-only.

14.3 Audit
Material actions log actor, time, action, record ID, source/reference, result, and relevant change summary. Audit logs avoid unnecessary sensitive data. Audit records are protected from user alteration.

14.4 Secrets
Secrets must not be embedded in source code, frontend bundles, logs, reports, or ordinary Sheets tables. Secret storage and rotation are controlled by Owner-approved procedures.

14.5 Privacy
Collect minimum necessary personal data. Define access, retention, export, correction, and deletion obligations according to law and Owner policy. Do not expose sensitive fields through broad API responses.

14.6 Security failure
Invalid signature, missing secret, unauthorized role, missing required response echoes, invalid schema, or suspicious request must fail closed and be logged safely.

====================================================================
15. ERROR, EXCEPTION, AND RECOVERY POLICY
====================================================================

For each failure class, define: detection; transaction state; user message; whether retry is safe; recovery method; accountable role; audit evidence.

15.1 Network/system unavailable
Show unavailable state. Do not silently use local demo data. Prevent unverified writes. Recovery requires reconnection and safe transaction-status check.

15.2 Timeout after submission
Treat as UNKNOWN outcome, not failure or success. Retain nonce/correlation. Query status or reconcile before retrying.

15.3 Duplicate submission
Idempotency must prevent duplicate effect. Return original result or explicit conflict/recovery response.

15.4 Partial transaction
Use atomicity where feasible. Otherwise record partial state and compensating/recovery steps. Do not leave hidden partial stock or finance effects.

15.5 Invalid/missing data
Reject with actionable validation. Do not auto-fill invented business values.

15.6 Discrepancy
Create/retain exception with severity, source, owner, date, reason, evidence, resolution, and approval as applicable.

15.7 Safe rollback
Posted transactions are corrected through linked reversal/adjustment. Rollback must not erase evidence or recycle IDs.

19. ACCEPTANCE AND COMPLETION STANDARD
====================================================================

A requirement is complete only when its acceptance criteria and required evidence are satisfied.

Completion evidence levels:
E0 — Requirement documented.
E1 — Source code or configuration inspected.
E2 — Static automated evidence produced.
E3 — Unit/integration/workflow tests passed.
E4 — Runtime verification completed in controlled environment.
E5 — Live integration evidence obtained under explicit authorization.
E6 — Owner acceptance recorded.

The requirement specifies which levels are necessary. Not every requirement needs E5, but critical governed transactions, security controls, and live integration must not be declared production-ready based only on E1/E2.

Acceptance checklist:
- Requirement is in the approved register.
- Business policy is defined.
- Calculation and edge cases documented, if applicable.
- Table/API/action contract is mapped.
- Role and approval controls defined.
- UI and error states are addressed.
- Source implementation is present.
- Required verification evidence exists.
- Dependencies are resolved or explicitly accepted.
- No known critical/high defects remain.
- Owner acceptance recorded when required.

Release readiness:
- No unresolved critical security/data integrity defect.
- No uncontrolled business-write path.
- No duplicate posting risk without mitigation.
- No unknown outcome incorrectly reported as success.
- Core stock and accounting reconciliation passes.
- Schema and policies are approved.
- Backup and rollback plan is valid.
- Role permissions reviewed.
- Required professional policy gates cleared.
- Owner explicitly approves release.

25. DEVELOPMENT FREEZE ON TESTING, VERIFICATION, AND RELEASE CHECKS
====================================================================

25.1 Absolute testing moratorium during development
No testing, verification, validation runs, builds, runtime checks, or release checks may be performed until the complete planned development implementation is finished across all in-scope ERP modules and workstreams.

This restriction applies to, but is not limited to:
- Unit tests, integration tests, end-to-end tests, automated test suites, and manual workflow testing.
- npm/pnpm/yarn test, pytest, or equivalent test runners.
- Build or bundler commands (build, bundle, compile).
- Type checking (tsc or equivalent).
- Linting, formatting, static analyzers, and code-quality checks.
- Python compilation checks such as py_compile.
- Runtime server start, local HTTP calls, browser interaction tests, or bridge round trips.
- Test writes to Google Sheets, Apps Script execution against business tables, test data creation in any business database, or live integration verification.
- Performance/load tests, security tests, migration checks, cutover checks, or go-live checks.

25.2 Allowed development work before testing phase
- Reading and inspecting provided source code and documentation to understand requirements.
- Planning and implementation of code, UI, backend, Apps Script, policy configuration, documentation, and source-level corrections.
- Reviewing implementation reports and code changes for scope alignment, without running tests or validation tools.
- Preparing test plans, test cases, acceptance criteria, test datasets for isolated future use, and release checklists as documentation only. These artifacts must not be executed.
- Recording blockers, decisions, and known limitations.

25.3 Definition of “complete development”
Development is considered complete for the planned upgrade when:
- Every in-scope requirement in the approved Requirement Register has an implementation disposition (implemented, explicitly deferred, or Owner-approved out of scope).
- All modules/workstreams and required integration contracts have implementation coverage.
- All approved workflows, policy rules, calculations, tables, roles, API actions, screens, and reports have been implemented or explicitly dispositioned.
- All known development blockers have been resolved or explicitly accepted as deferred by Owner.
- The development handover report and source checkpoint are complete.
- No testing has been executed during this period.

25.4 Testing phase entry gate
Testing begins only after the Owner explicitly authorizes it in a separate message. At that point, the project may use the approved test plan in controlled environments. An Owner statement that development is complete is not automatically testing authorization unless it explicitly authorizes testing.

25.5 Test sequence after authorization
When authorized, testing will be planned in controlled sequence: static/source verification; schema and contract checks; calculation tests; unit tests; integration tests; workflow tests; RBAC/security checks; failure/duplicate/timeout recovery; reconciliation tests; controlled staging/live integration only with explicit separate authorization; release acceptance. Test failures are logged, corrected, and retested only after testing authorization remains active.

25.6 Never misreport
Before the test phase, status may be “IMPLEMENTED — NOT TESTED” or “DEVELOPMENT COMPLETE — TESTING NOT AUTHORIZED.” Do not label code as tested, verified, validated, production-ready, or released.

0. DOCUMENT CONTROL, AUTHORITY AND INTERPRETATION
======================================================================

0.1 Purpose
This blueprint is the master business and database requirement document for Kawad Swad.
It specifies what the Google Sheets database must record, how operational and financial
transactions must be controlled, which reports must be produced, and how Apps Script
may safely support those processes.

It is written for the Owner, accounts, procurement, stores, production, packaging,
quality, sales, distribution, workforce administration and authorised technical support.
Factory-facing instructions and forms must use simple language.

0.2 Authority hierarchy
After Owner adoption, use this order when documents or instructions conflict:
1. Applicable law and binding government directions, as confirmed for Kawad Swad.
2. This Owner-approved Master Database & Policy Blueprint.
3. The approved Data Dictionary and Table Register derived from this blueprint.
4. The approved Accounting Policy, approval matrix and department procedures.
5. The approved Google Sheets workbook structure and versioned Apps Script release.
6. Training notes, user guides and informal working instructions.

A spreadsheet tab, formula, script or old practice does not override an approved policy.
A code feature does not become an approved business rule merely because it exists.
If a legal or tax question is unresolved, mark it PENDING PROFESSIONAL VERIFICATION;
do not invent a rule or let the script silently decide it.

0.3 Source-of-truth rule
Google Sheets is the authoritative business database for Kawad Swad's core business,
operational, inventory, sales and accounting records.
Apps Script is the approved automation layer for validation, numbering, calculations,
workflow assistance, reports and controlled writes. It must not create an independent
competing source of truth.
A calculated dashboard or cached value is not the original transaction record.
A document stored in Drive is supporting evidence and must be linked to its business record.

0.4 Scope boundary
This blueprint governs:
- Daily manufacturing and factory operations.
- Raw materials, procurement, suppliers and inventory.
- Milling, production, quality, packaging and finished goods.
- Sales, customers, invoices, receipts, dispatch and distribution.
- Employee administration, attendance, wages and incentives.
- Expenses, costing, accounting, cash, bank, tax and financial statements.
- Compliance evidence, ethics, approvals, audit, documents and continuity.
- Google Sheets table design, data dictionary, Apps Script controls and reports.

This blueprint does not independently authorise work on GitHub, external backend architecture,
front-end development, cloud deployment, data restoration, historic-data import or live
spreadsheet modification. Such work is outside this scope unless the Owner separately asks.

0.5 No automatic implementation authority
A requirement in this document means the system must eventually support it. It does not mean
that a worksheet, column, formula, ID range or script may be changed immediately.
Every live schema or workflow change requires a reviewed change request, tested copy,
impact assessment, backup, rollback plan and explicit Owner approval.

0.6 Requirement status vocabulary
REQUIRED: The system must support the capability.
DEFINED: The business meaning and minimum record content are specified.
PENDING OWNER DECISION: The Owner must choose the business policy or threshold.
PENDING CA / PROFESSIONAL VERIFICATION: A qualified adviser must confirm the treatment.
PENDING WORKBOOK MAPPING: The existing physical sheet/column must be matched to the requirement.
PENDING SCRIPT VALIDATION: Apps Script implementation and test evidence are outstanding.
LIVE-APPROVED: May be used in the live workbook only after explicit release approval.

0.7 Design principle: logical records are not automatically separate tabs
The 115 requirements in Section 4 are capabilities and controls, not an instruction to create
115 separate worksheets. Related records may be stored in one well-designed table or in
separate header/detail/movement tables where that is necessary for reliable accounting.
The approved Table Register decides the final physical worksheets.
Do not create duplicate tables merely to mirror every report or screen.

1. NON-NEGOTIABLE BUSINESS AND DATABASE POLICIES
======================================================================

POL-001 - Google Sheets authority
Core business records must be stored in the approved Google Sheets database. Any secondary
copy or calculation must be reconcilable to those records. A backup is a protected copy,
not an alternative place for routine transaction entry.

POL-002 - One record, one permanent identity
Each master and transaction record must have a unique, immutable ID. Row numbers, names,
phone numbers, dates and SKU descriptions must not be used as permanent primary keys.
IDs must never be recycled, including after cancellation or archival.

POL-003 - No silent data loss
A transaction must not be reported as successful if a mandatory field or related record
was not saved and verified. Missing headers, invalid references, invalid units or failed
writes must create a clear failure or controlled pending exception.

POL-004 - Append-only posted history
Posted financial entries, stock movements, receipts, payments, production outputs and
other material business events must not be silently overwritten or deleted. Correct them
through linked reversal, credit/debit note or adjustment records with reason and approval.

POL-005 - Every stock change has a movement
A stock balance must be explainable from receipts, issues, consumption, production output,
transfers, dispatches, returns, damages, expiry and approved adjustments. A user must not
simply type a new quantity over a ledger-derived balance.

POL-006 - Every money change has a financial record
Money received, paid, earned, owed, refunded, transferred, advanced or adjusted must have
an identifiable transaction, account treatment, source document and payment evidence
where applicable.

POL-007 - Separate physical, commercial and financial events
A purchase order is not a receipt. A receipt is not a supplier invoice. An invoice is not
payment. Dispatch is not proof of delivery. Marketplace delivered status is not proof of
settlement to the bank. The database must preserve each event separately and link them.

POL-008 - Double-entry accounting
Posted financial transactions must create balanced debit and credit journal lines where
double-entry accounting applies. Journal totals must balance by voucher and accounting
period. Directly editing ledger balances is prohibited.

POL-009 - Source-document traceability
Invoices, receipts, supplier bills, bank references, production records, approvals and
other evidence must be linked to the originating transaction using stable IDs and document
references. A file attachment alone does not prove a transaction happened.

POL-010 - Effective-dated master data
Recipes, prices, tax classifications, label versions, supplier terms, credit limits,
approval limits and other changing policies must have effective dates and version history.
Historical transactions retain the version/rate that applied when they were posted.

POL-011 - Segregation of duties
Where practical, request, approval, payment release, cash custody, reconciliation and
quality release must not all be controlled by one person. Exceptions require reason,
authorised approver and post-event review.

POL-012 - Least-privilege access
Users may access only the information needed for their responsibilities. Employee salary,
identity, bank, personal documents and internal policy information are restricted to the
Owner and specifically authorised roles. Sensitive information must not appear in general
reports, shared screens or unrestricted logs.

POL-013 - No fabricated records
No user or script may invent attendance, production, quality checks, receipts, payments,
delivery evidence, tax filings or approvals. Unknown data must be marked unknown or pending.

POL-014 - Reconciliation is mandatory
Cash, bank, supplier, customer, inventory, marketplace settlement, GST support and control
accounts must be reconciled at defined frequencies. Differences must be recorded in an
exception register and resolved with evidence.

POL-015 - Simple operational language
Factory and sales data-entry labels must be understandable to the people recording them.
Accounting terms may be retained in accounts reports with a plain-language explanation.

POL-016 - No assumption becomes policy
Tax treatment, valuation method, costing basis, credit terms, wage rules, approval thresholds,
legal applicability and retention period require Owner or professional confirmation.
The database may support configurable rules, but must not hard-code unverified assumptions.

POL-017 - Empty-database and historical-data boundary
The current approved business database baseline is to be treated as the active clean database
unless the Owner separately approves otherwise. Historical pre-reset records and repair
previews are archive/reference material only. Do not restore, re-import, regenerate or reserve
historic business IDs merely because an old report or backup contains them.

POL-018 - Controlled live changes
No live sheet header, table, record, formula, validation or script may be modified by an
unreviewed bulk process. First inspect, map, test in a disposable copy, verify and obtain
explicit approval.

POL-019 - Owner decision prevails on commercial policy
The Owner approves product identity, selling prices, discounts, credit limits, business
roles, spending limits, stock adjustments, write-offs and other reserved business decisions.
A script may enforce approved rules; it must not create policy on its own.

POL-020 - Current applicable law prevails
The database supports compliance evidence. It does not determine legal applicability.
A qualified CA, food-safety adviser, labour/legal adviser or other professional must confirm
obligations for the actual entity, premises, activity, turnover, employees and current rules.

2. BUSINESS MODEL AND END-TO-END OPERATING FLOW
======================================================================

2.1 Master-data setup
Owner approves business identity, factories/locations, products, variants, SKUs, units,
recipes, suppliers, customers, sales channels, price lists, tax setup, payment methods,
expense categories, chart of accounts, user roles and approval limits.

2.2 Procurement-to-stock flow
Material requirement -> purchase request -> supplier quotation (where used) -> approval ->
purchase order -> goods receipt -> incoming quantity/quality check -> accepted/rejected /
quarantined status -> supplier bill -> payable -> payment -> allocation -> supplier statement
reconciliation.

2.3 Manufacturing flow
Daily factory opening -> production plan/order -> approved recipe/version -> material lot
selection -> material issue -> milling/preparation where applicable -> production batch ->
actual consumption -> output and process loss -> in-process/finished quality check -> hold,
release or rejection -> production cost calculation -> bulk finished stock.

2.4 Packaging flow
Packaging plan -> source bulk lot -> packaging-material issue -> packaging batch -> packet
count and net quantity checks -> damaged packets/loss -> approved label version -> quality
release -> finished SKU lot -> finished-goods stock.

2.5 Sales-to-cash flow
Enquiry/order -> customer/channel reference -> price and tax check -> credit/payment approval
-> stock reservation -> pick/pack -> invoice -> dispatch -> delivery/return -> receipt or
marketplace/gateway settlement -> payment allocation -> bank/cash reconciliation -> close.

2.6 Employee-to-payroll flow
Employee/worker master -> approved role and wage basis -> attendance/shift -> work assignment
or output record -> leave/overtime/incentive approval -> payroll calculation -> payment
approval -> payment evidence -> employee balance reconciliation.

2.7 Expense-to-payment flow
Expense request or bill -> category/cost centre -> supporting document -> approval -> expense
voucher -> cash/bank payment -> accounting posting -> tax treatment where applicable -> review.

2.8 Financial close flow
Check source records -> complete postings -> reconcile cash/bank/parties/stock/tax/control
accounts -> investigate exceptions -> trial balance -> adjustments with approval -> financial
statements -> close period -> lock posted period -> preserve archive and opening balances.

2.9 Transaction states
Use controlled states as appropriate:
DRAFT -> SUBMITTED -> APPROVED -> POSTED -> RECONCILED -> CLOSED.
Other states may include REJECTED, CANCELLED, ON_HOLD, PARTIALLY_PAID, PARTIALLY_RECEIVED,
PARTIALLY_DISPATCHED, RETURNED and REVERSED.
Every allowed transition must specify who may perform it and which fields/evidence are required.
A cancelled or rejected transaction must remain traceable; do not delete its identity.

3. GOOGLE SHEETS DATABASE DESIGN STANDARD
======================================================================

3.1 Required database layers
A. Master tables: relatively stable entities such as Products, Suppliers, Customers, Workers,
   Units, Locations, Accounts, Expense Categories and Payment Methods.
B. Transaction header tables: one row per purchase, receipt, production order, invoice,
   payment, expense voucher or journal voucher.
C. Transaction detail tables: one row per line item, material, expense split, invoice item,
   allocation or journal line.
D. Movement/ledger tables: append-only records of stock, cash, bank, party balances and
   other events.
E. Control tables: approvals, IDs, exceptions, audit events, status history and configuration.
F. Document register: metadata and links to evidence stored in approved Drive locations.
G. Reporting views: derived summaries; never the only place where a transaction is recorded.

3.2 Standard fields
Each table must define only the fields appropriate to its record grain. Common fields should
be included where relevant:
- record_id: immutable unique ID.
- record_status: controlled status.
- created_at and created_by.
- updated_at and updated_by, where updates are allowed.
- submitted_at / submitted_by, where workflow requires submission.
- approved_at / approved_by, where approval is required.
- posted_at / posted_by, for posted financial or stock records.
- source_type and source_record_id, to identify the originating transaction.
- document_id or evidence reference, where supporting evidence exists.
- reversal_of_id or adjustment_of_id, where correcting a previous record.
- effective_from / effective_to, for versioned master data.
- idempotency_key or request_reference, for retry-safe automated transactions.

3.3 Header and data-type policy
Every physical table must have an approved header definition in the Data Dictionary.
Header names must be explicit, unique and stable. Apps Script must map by header name, not
by assumed column position. Duplicate or blank headers are a schema exception, not an
invitation to guess. Date, date-time, text, integer, decimal, boolean and JSON/text fields
must have documented types, formats and validation.

3.4 Numeric and financial data
Money must use exact decimal handling and a documented currency (INR unless formally changed).
Do not use binary floating-point logic for financial decisions where decimal-safe handling
is available. Store quantity and unit explicitly. Define conversion factors and rounding rules.
GST rate, taxable value, tax amount, discount, freight, fee and net amount must be separate
fields wherever needed to support accurate invoices and reconciliation.

3.5 Relationships
Every foreign reference must point to an approved record ID. Examples:
- SupplierBill -> Supplier and PurchaseOrder/GoodsReceipt where applicable.
- SupplierPayment -> Supplier and one or more PaymentAllocations.
- SalesInvoice -> Customer/Channel and SalesOrder where applicable.
- CustomerReceipt -> Customer and ReceiptAllocations.
- MaterialIssue -> ProductionBatch and RawMaterialLot.
- ProductionBatch -> RecipeVersion and source material lots.
- PackagingBatch -> BulkProductionLot and PackagingMaterialLots.
- FinishedGoodsLot -> PackagingBatch and ProductSKU.
- Dispatch -> SalesInvoice/Order and FinishedGoodsLot.
- JournalEntry -> source transaction, accounting period and JournalEntryLines.

3.6 One-to-many design
Where a transaction can have multiple products, suppliers' bill items, payments, allocations,
materials, employees or journal lines, use separate detail tables. Do not store variable
lists in a single cell as the only authoritative record. A JSON snapshot may be kept as a
supporting payload only if the individual records remain queryable and reconcilable.

3.7 Derived balances
Cash, bank, customer, supplier, stock, loan and control-account balances should be calculated
from posted transactions or maintained as explicitly reconcilable snapshots. A manually
edited balance must never replace its supporting ledger.

3.8 Master data versioning
Maintain change history/effective dates for products, SKUs, recipes, recipe ingredients,
suppliers' approved terms, customer credit limits, price lists, tax classifications, labels,
expense categories, approval limits and chart-of-account changes. Historical transactions
must retain their original commercial and tax snapshots.

3.9 Workbook organisation
The final workbook may contain multiple tabs grouped by department. Tab names must be stable,
short, meaningful and unique. A Table Register must identify for each physical tab:
- Table ID and exact tab name.
- Business purpose and record grain.
- Owner department and accountable role.
- Primary key and unique constraints.
- Required/optional fields and data types.
- Foreign-key relationships.
- Source and downstream tables.
- Read/write permissions.
- Posting/movement rules.
- Reports using the table.
- Retention and sensitivity classification.
- Apps Script functions using the table.
- Current implementation and approval status.

3.10 Do not create tables solely for reports
Dashboards and reports should normally be calculated from validated source tables. Store a
snapshot only where a formal period close or approved audit requirement needs an immutable
report copy. Identify snapshots as snapshots, not source transactions.

REQ-017 | Trial Balance

REQ-019 | Invoice recorder

REQ-020 | Receipt record
Required capability: receipt voucher/register for customer, marketplace, bank, cash and other
receipts, including receipt mode, date, amount, reference, allocation/unallocated amount,
evidence and reversal/refund link.

6. ACCOUNTING POLICIES AND POSTING CONTROL
======================================================================

6.1 Chart of Accounts policy
The Chart of Accounts must be established before routine journal automation. It must contain:
- Account code and account name.
- Account class: Asset, Liability, Equity/Capital, Income, Cost of Sales, Expense or other
  CA-approved class.
- Parent/group and reporting line.
- Normal balance (debit or credit).
- Control account flag and linked subledger.
- Whether manual journal posting is allowed.
- Tax/reporting mapping where applicable.
- Active/effective dates and approval history.

Suggested account groups (final codes and names require Owner/CA approval):
ASSETS: Cash on Hand; Bank Accounts; Trade Receivables; Supplier Advances; Inventory - Raw
Materials; Inventory - Work in Progress; Inventory - Finished Goods; Input Tax Credit where
applicable; Prepaid Expenses; Fixed Assets; Accumulated Depreciation; Deposits and Other Assets.
LIABILITIES: Trade Payables; Customer Advances; GST/Other Tax Payables; Wages Payable;
Expenses Payable; Loans; Other Current/Long-Term Liabilities.
EQUITY: Owner Capital; Owner Drawings; Retained Earnings/Accumulated Profit; Current Period
Profit or Loss, as prescribed by the approved accounting setup.
INCOME: Product Sales; Other Operating Income; Sales Returns/Allowances as contra-income;
Discounts as defined by the approved accounting policy.
COST OF SALES: Raw Material Consumed; Direct Production Labour; Manufacturing Overheads;
Packaging Cost; Inventory Cost of Goods Sold; Production Loss/Wastage as approved.
EXPENSES: Selling/Marketplace Fees; Freight/Delivery; Advertising; Office/Admin; Repairs and
Maintenance; Utilities; Rent; Payroll not included in production cost; Professional Fees; Bank
and Payment Gateway Charges; Depreciation; Interest; Other approved expenses.

Do not create duplicate accounts for every product or channel unless the approved reporting
model requires it. Product and channel analysis should normally use dimensions/cost centres.

6.2 Opening balance policy
Opening balances must be created only after an approved cut-off date and verified source.
Required categories may include:
- Cash by cash location/custodian.
- Bank by bank account and statement date.
- Customer-wise receivables by invoice/document.
- Supplier-wise payables by bill/document.
- Customer and supplier advances/unallocated balances.
- Raw material, WIP, bulk finished goods and packaged finished goods by item/lot/location.
- Fixed assets and accumulated depreciation.
- Loans and other liabilities.
- Owner capital and retained earnings/opening equity.
- Applicable tax balances and control accounts.

Every opening balance needs source, date, amount/quantity, account/party/item, method of
verification, prepared by, reviewed by and approval. The opening Trial Balance must balance.
Stock opening quantities must be supported by physical count or verified records and approved
valuation. Historic pre-reset data must not be imported merely to fill an opening balance.

6.3 General Journal and posting policy
A journal voucher must have unique ID/number, date, period, narration, source reference,
line items, debit/credit, cost centre/dimension where used, supporting document, requester,
approver and posting status.
A posted journal must satisfy:
- Total debit equals total credit.
- All accounts are active and postable.
- Date is in an open financial period.
- Required source/evidence and approval exist.
- Tax/party/stock-control rules are not bypassed.
- Posting succeeds and is read back and verified.

Manual journals to customer, supplier, stock, GST, cash or bank control accounts must be
restricted. Prefer the underlying subledger transaction. Any approved control-account journal
must include reason, supporting reconciliation and Owner/CA-authorised approval.

6.4 Core posting logic - policy templates
The following are conceptual controls. Exact account mapping and tax treatment must be
approved by the CA before implementation.

A. Cash sale / receipt of sale:
Debit Cash/Bank/Payment Clearing as applicable.
Credit Sales Revenue and applicable tax liability/other components according to invoice rules.

B. Credit sale:
Debit Trade Receivables.
Credit Sales Revenue and applicable tax liability/other invoice components.

C. Customer receipt against invoice:
Debit Cash/Bank/Payment Clearing.
Credit Trade Receivables. Any unallocated excess remains a separately identified customer
advance/unallocated receipt, not sales income a second time.

D. Purchase of inventory on supplier credit:
Debit appropriate Inventory/Expense/Tax Input accounts according to receipt, invoice and
approved accounting treatment.
Credit Trade Payables.

E. Supplier payment:
Debit Trade Payables or Supplier Advance, according to allocation.
Credit Cash/Bank. Payment does not create a second purchase expense.

F. Supplier advance:
Debit Supplier Advances.
Credit Cash/Bank. Apply to supplier bill only through an approved allocation.

G. Material issue to production:
Debit Work in Progress/Production Cost account as configured.
Credit Raw Material Inventory. Link material lot and production batch.

H. Production completion:
Debit Bulk Finished Goods/Finished Goods or approved WIP transfer account.
Credit Work in Progress, using approved accumulated batch cost.

I. Packaging conversion:
Transfer bulk product and packaging conversion costs to packaged finished-goods inventory
according to the approved costing design.

J. Dispatch/sale of inventory:
Recognise Cost of Goods Sold and reduce inventory using the approved valuation method at the
accounting recognition point defined by the CA-approved policy.

K. Expense paid immediately:
Debit appropriate expense/prepayment/asset account.
Credit Cash/Bank. If unpaid, credit the appropriate payable instead.

L. Bank/cash transfer:
Debit receiving cash/bank account and credit the sending account. A transfer is not income
or expense.

M. Return/refund/credit note:
Use a linked reversal/credit note and related stock inspection/disposition and tax adjustment.
Never delete or edit the original invoice/receipt to hide the return.

6.5 Sales and purchase recognition
The database must distinguish order, invoice, receipt, dispatch, delivery, supplier receipt,
supplier bill and payment. The Owner/CA-approved policy must specify the accounting recognition
point for each transaction type and relevant applicable tax point. A workflow status alone
must not be treated as proof of accounting recognition.

6.6 Receivable and payable policy
Maintain document-level balances, due dates, payment terms, allocations, credit/debit notes,
disputes, advances and ageing. Unallocated cash must remain visible. Reconcile customer and
supplier subledgers to General Ledger control accounts at each close.

6.7 Cash and bank policy
Cash custody must identify location and responsible custodian. Each operating day must have
opening cash, receipts, payments, deposits/withdrawals, physical closing count and variance
explanation. Bank book entries must be matched to bank statement lines. Unmatched items must
remain open and assigned.

6.8 Expense policy
An expense record must state date, payee, purpose, category, cost centre, amount, tax fields
where applicable, payment mode, evidence, requester, approver and accounting status.
Personal expenses must not be mixed with business expenses. Owner drawings/reimbursements
must be recorded distinctly. Reimbursements need original proof and approval.

6.9 Profit policy
The system must present separate measures:
- Gross profit: net revenue less cost of goods sold, on the approved accounting basis.
- Contribution: net selling value excluding applicable taxes collected on behalf of government,
  less directly attributable production, packaging, freight, channel, payment, discount, return
  and other direct costs as defined.
- Operating profit: contribution/gross profit less the allocated/recorded operating expenses
  under the approved reporting method.
- Net profit: profit after finance costs, depreciation, tax and other required items under the
  approved accounting basis.

Management contribution reports are not a substitute for formal P&L. Every report must state
whether it is actual, standard, estimated or allocated.

6.10 Inventory valuation policy
Before production-cost automation, the Owner and CA must approve the inventory valuation
method, cost layers, treatment of freight, duties/taxes, normal/abnormal losses, by-products,
WIP, finished goods, returns and write-offs. The system must retain quantity and value movement
history and permit reconciliation to physical inventory and accounting control accounts.

6.11 Financial period policy
Define fiscal year start/end, period status, posting cut-off, adjustment window, closing review,
statement approval and new-year opening. Closed periods are read-only except through a
controlled reopening/adjustment process with Owner/CA approval and audit trail.

7. DAILY OPERATING POLICIES
======================================================================

7.1 Factory daily opening
The authorised supervisor records date, shift, staff presence, active orders, machine readiness,
raw material availability, pending quality holds and opening stock/checklist. Missing checks
must be shown as incomplete, not auto-marked complete.

7.2 Daily production
Every batch must identify product/variant, approved recipe version, planned quantity, material
lots, material issued, actual consumed, workers, supervisor, timestamps, output, process loss,
quality checks and status. Rework must be separately recorded and linked to the source batch.

7.3 Daily production closing
Reconcile raw material issue/return, WIP, bulk output, loss, quality hold, released quantity and
closing balances. Supervisor explains variances and submits closing. Any variance above the
Owner-approved tolerance requires approval.

7.4 Daily packaging
Record source bulk lot, input weight/quantity, SKU/pack size, packaging materials, output packet
count/net weight checks, rejected/damaged packets, residual bulk and packaging loss. Finished
stock is saleable only after required checks and release.

7.5 Daily sales
Capture every order from website, marketplace, phone/WhatsApp, direct, retailer, distributor,
institutional, exhibition and factory counter where active. Preserve channel reference. Validate
product, approved price version, discount, taxes, stock and credit/payment requirements.

7.6 Daily sales closing
Reconcile orders, invoices, cancellations, dispatches, returns, cash, UPI, bank, gateway and
marketplace collection records. Unpaid credit sales remain receivables. Marketplace settlement
is reconciled separately from order value. Count physical counter stock and record samples,
damage and returns.

7.7 Daily employee handling
Record attendance/absence, shift, approved overtime, work assignment and production activity.
Corrections require an attributable approval. Only verified data feeds wage/payroll calculations.

7.8 Daily cash closing
Record opening float, each receipt/payment, deposit/withdrawal, physical cash count, calculated
cash, difference, explanation, custodian and reviewer. Shortage/overage cannot be hidden by
editing the opening figure or deleting a voucher.

7.9 Daily expense handling
Small expenses must still have a voucher, category, purpose, payee, date, proof where available,
approval and payment record. Petty cash is reconciled to physical cash and replenishment.

7.10 Daily exception review
Owner/authorised manager reviews critical incomplete writes, stock variance, quality holds,
unmatched payment, failed delivery, overdue collection, unauthorised discount and approval
queue. Every exception has an assigned owner and due date.

8.5 No unsupported payment success

completion status and external reference. Payment posting/clearing and bank reconciliation
must be distinct statuses where applicable.

9.3 Food safety

9.5 Labour and workplace

9.6 Privacy and confidentiality
Collect only necessary personal data. Restrict employee and customer records by purpose and

Maintain incident and access-review records.

9.7 Ethics

11. GOOGLE APPS SCRIPT DEVELOPMENT POLICY
======================================================================

11.1 Purpose
Apps Script must implement approved business rules and reduce manual error. It is not the
policy owner. A script must never decide an unresolved business rule by guessing.

11.2 Development rules
- Use the approved Data Dictionary and Table Register as the only schema specification.
- Map columns by exact approved header names; do not depend on column number.
- Validate all required fields before write.
- Validate master references, units, status transitions, dates, permissions and financial rules.
- Create unique IDs through the approved central allocator and check uniqueness.
- Use a retry-safe request key/idempotency control for critical writes.
- Use locking/serialization suitable for all script executions and avoid concurrent duplicate IDs.
- Write multi-table transactions with a durable operation state or recovery log.
- Read back critical writes and verify every required field and related record.
- If any required step fails, do not report success. Preserve pending/failed state and recovery details.
- Log actor, time, function, record ID, action and outcome without exposing secrets or unnecessary PII.
- Keep configuration and business thresholds separate from source code where safe and approved.
- Use clear user-facing messages and explain the next safe action.

11.3 Prohibited script behaviours
Scripts must not silently:
- Create or rename business tabs or headers in the live workbook.
- Delete, clear, reorder or rewrite historical records.
- Change a posted invoice, payment, stock or journal event in place.
- Generate opening balances from old backup data.
- Restore pre-reset records or import old repair proposals.
- Allocate IDs for unapproved transactions.
- Bypass approval because a cell is manually changed to APPROVED.
- Mark a failed write as complete.
- Treat a marketplace order total as a bank settlement.
- Treat an order as an invoice or a payment initiation as cleared cash.
- Change prices, tax rates, costing policy, wage rates or approval thresholds without approval.

11.4 Menu and form policy
Menus, forms and sidebars must expose only role-authorised actions. High-risk operations must
show a confirmation summary and require approval. User-facing controls must not reveal
restricted information through search results, errors or hidden columns.

11.5 Script logging and exceptions
Every critical script function must record operation ID, user, timestamp, input reference,
write targets, result, verification result and exception details. Logs must not include
passwords, access tokens or full bank credentials. Failed operations must appear in an
Exception Register with a safe retry/recovery path.

11.6 Script testing
Test each change in a disposable workbook copy using:
- Valid record.
- Missing mandatory field.
- Invalid type/unit/reference.
- Duplicate request/retry.
- Concurrent execution where relevant.
- Partial write and recovery.
- Insufficient permission.
- Closed financial period.
- Reversal/cancellation case.
- Reconciliation and expected report result.

11.7 Release gate
No live Apps Script deployment/execution is allowed until schema mapping, owner approval,
backup, test evidence, rollback, user permissions and post-release verification are recorded.

3. NON-NEGOTIABLE ARCHITECTURE & LAYER OWNERSHIP
======================================================================

3.1 Authoritative record system & execution boundaries
- Google Sheets (255 verified tabs) is the sole authoritative business
  database (`POL-001`).
- **Apps Script (Single Write & Posting Owner):** Apps Script owns all
  governed mutations, `LockService`-serialized ID allocation (`IdAllocator.gs`),
  `nonce`-based idempotency (`SafeWriter.gs`), stock overdraft validation
  (`InventoryEngine.gs`), QC hold enforcement, paise-exact double-entry
  journal posting (`AccountingEngine.gs`), workforce payout separation
  (`WorkforceEngine.gs`), and 12-column `AuditLog` entries.
- **FastAPI (Read, Query, Auth & Gateway Layer):** FastAPI authenticates
  users, enforces role/field-level read permissions, serves paginated and
  filtered list queries from Google Sheets, and routes all state-changing
  business writes through governed Apps Script Web App / Execution API
  endpoints. FastAPI must never independently allocate IDs or append raw
  ledger rows outside Apps Script's `LockService` and read-back verification.
- **React 18 + TypeScript + Vite (UI Layer):** Displays records fetched via
  FastAPI and submits mutations with client-generated idempotency `nonce` keys.
- **Browser Storage:** Restricted strictly to non-authoritative UI preferences
  (e.g., sidebar state) and session auth tokens. Never stores business records.

3.2 Prohibited architecture
- Do not introduce MongoDB, SQLite, PostgreSQL or another database as a
  competing or authoritative store for business transactions.
- Do not silently write to both Sheets and a second database.
- Do not allow frontend `localStorage`, `initialData.ts` or in-memory fallback
  data to masquerade as saved production data (`POL-003`, `POL-013`).
- Do not bypass Apps Script `LockService`, `nonce` idempotency, or read-back
  verification for protected transactions.
- Do not re-audit or recreate the 46 deleted build-phase scratch tabs.

3.3 Transaction safety
- Every write request from React must carry a unique client `nonce` (matching
  `makeNonce()` in `ErpSidebar.html`) for retry-safe execution on unstable
  mobile connections.
- Separate request, approval, posting, payment, dispatch, settlement and
  reversal states (`POL-007`).
- Posted financial and stock history is append-only; corrections use linked
  reversals/adjustments (`POL-004`).
- Money calculations use integer paise (`Math.round(val * 100)`) in Apps Script
  and exact `Decimal` types in FastAPI Pydantic schemas (`POL-008`).
- Stock balances derive exclusively from validated `InventoryMovements` rows
  (`POL-005`).

## PART III: STANDARD OPERATING PROCEDURES (SOP) BY DEPARTMENT

### 1. Role-Based Access Matrix (`Users` Tab)
Every staff member must be registered in the **`Users`** tab with their exact Google Workspace/Gmail address and one of the following active roles:
* **`OWNER_ADMIN`**: Full access across all 6 sidebar forms, management reports, schema governance, and confidential payroll (`Gig` + `Fixed` employees).
* **`PA`**: Purchase, Production, Sales, Finance (Receipts/Expenses), and Management Reports.
* **`STORE` / `PROCUREMENT`**: Form `1. Purchase / Material Receipt`.
* **`PRODUCTION`**: Form `2. Production Batch Entry` and supervisor verification of worker entries.
* **`QUALITY`**: Form `3. Quality Release / Hold` (`RELEASED`, `ON_HOLD`, `REJECTED`).
* **`PACKAGING`**: Form `4. Packaging Batch (SKUs)` (200g, 500g, 1000g retail packs).
* **`SALES` / `FIELD_SALESMAN`**: Form `5. Sales Order & Invoice` and `6. Customer Receipt` (for field collections).
* **`ACCOUNTS`**: Form `6. Customer Receipt / Expense`, Journal Vouchers, and Management Reports.
* **`AUDITOR`**: Read-only access to reports, ledgers, and audit logs.

### 2. Daily Operational Workflow (End-to-End Traceability)
1. **Raw & Packaging Material Receipt (Store):** Open `Kawad Swad ERP` $\rightarrow$ `1. Purchase`. Enter Material ID, Warehouse (`WH-MAIN`), Quantity, Unit, Rate, and set Initial QC Status to `PENDING_QC` (or `RELEASED` if pre-inspected). System generates an immutable `LOT-*` and `MOV-*` ID.
2. **Production Batch Execution (Production Supervisor):** Open `2. Production`. Enter Product ID, Recipe Version ID, Source Raw Material Lot ID, Consumed KG, Bulk Output KG, and Process Loss KG. System blocks overdrafts (`POL-005`), issues raw material stock, creates a bulk lot in `PENDING_QC`, and records process loss.
3. **Quality Inspection & Lot Release (Quality Officer):** Open `3. Quality`. Enter the Bulk Lot ID, inspection parameter (`MOISTURE_AND_SENSORY`), measured reading, and select `RELEASED`. Packaging cannot use any lot until Quality marks it `RELEASED` (`POL-011`).
4. **Retail SKU Packaging (Packaging Team):** Open `4. Packaging`. Enter Packaging Batch No., Released Bulk Lot ID, Product ID, SKU Code, Packet Size (`200g`, `500g`, or `1000g`), and Good Packets Count. System deducts exact net weight from the bulk lot and generates a `RELEASED` Finished Goods SKU Lot.
5. **Sales Order, Invoice & Collection (Sales / Accounts):** Open `5. Sales` to capture Customer ID, Channel, SKU, Quantity, and Rate (with optional instant Sales Invoice). Open `6. Finance` to record Customer Receipts (auto-allocating against invoices) or Daily Expense Vouchers (auto-posting balanced Debit/Credit journal lines).
6. **Workforce & Dual Payroll (Supervisor + Owner):**
   * **Gig / Piece-Rate Workers:** Supervisor logs batch output via `ksRecordWorkerProductionEntry` and verifies via `ksVerifyWorkerProductionEntry`. Owner runs `ksProcessGigWorkerPayout` to settle verified entries and post labour expense.
   * **Fixed-Salary Employees:** Advances recorded via `ksRecordEmployeeAdvance`; monthly salary + overtime minus advance recovery processed by Owner via `ksProcessFixedEmployeeSalary`.
7. **Daily & Monthly Closing (Accounts / Owner):** Click `Kawad Swad ERP` $\rightarrow$ `Management Reports` $\rightarrow$ `Refresh All Core Reports` to verify `RPT_TrialBalance` (`BALANCED`), `RPT_StockStatus`, and `RPT_SalesAndReceivables`.
